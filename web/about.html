About Historica

I wanted to see what was happening in different parts of the world at the same time, without having to piece it together from separate accounts.

Why I’m building it

If I’m reading about Socrates, I want to know what was happening in China during his lifetime. If I’m looking at Alexander’s campaigns in Bactria, I want to see where Bactria was and compare it with the present-day map.

Historica is built around that idea. At any time you’re looking at one person, place, period or event. The timeline shows what else overlaps, and you can follow a connection to change the focus.

It’s for anyone who’s curious. I don’t intend it to be another Wikipedia or a reference for academics, but the information should have linked sources so you can see where it comes from.

What’s here so far

The longer-term aim is to cover all of human history. The current collection concentrates on roughly 3000 BCE to 200 CE, with some earlier material for predynastic Egypt and context outside those dates.

There are quite a lot of gaps. A place with few entries isn’t a place where nothing happened; it’s somewhere we haven’t covered properly yet.

Some dates are approximate or disputed, and a marker on the map doesn’t necessarily describe a territory. The notes on each page explain what the evidence supports and where there’s uncertainty.

Research and source checks use AI assistance. That isn’t independent expert review, and linking a source doesn’t mean we’ve finished checking everything in an entry. The research coverage page explains the distinction.

Work in progress

This is a beta. There’s more data to add, and I’m still working out how best to show it. The plan is to make the project open source once the prototype is ready; the repository is private for now.
