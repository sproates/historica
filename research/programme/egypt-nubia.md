# Egypt and Nubia expansion

24 November 2026. Eight new or expanded entries connect the Middle Kingdom and Kerma with places, records and material evidence. This is a bounded addition, not completion of either region's research coverage.

Expanded: Egypt's Middle Kingdom, Senwosret III's reign and the kingdom of Kerma.

Added: Amenemhat III's reign, Lahun, work and accounts at Lahun, Abydos in the Middle Kingdom, and a Kerma beaker found at Abydos.

## Evidence and interpretation

Sources and passage references are recorded with each claim in the Egyptian and early-world evidence packs. The passages were opened and inspected during this task. Review is AI-assisted source checking, not independent expert review.

- Reign dates follow the existing Met chronology. UCL's alternative for Senwosret III and the Egyptian ministry's alternative for Amenemhat III are retained in visible notes. The UCL Amenemhat III page has an internally inconsistent heading date, so only its building account is used.
- Lahun's 1850–1700 BCE window follows UCL's town evidence. Its accounts are not asserted to span that entire interval. Temple papers and town manuscripts remain distinct groups.
- Abydos has an explicitly editorial Middle Kingdom window. Neither that window nor Lahun's describes the settlement's entire existence.
- The beaker's interval is an approximate object date. Its map point represents its find area in Egypt, not its manufacture in Nubia. The owner's identity and journey remain unknown.
- Kerma's point comes from Sudan's 2022 UNESCO tentative-list submission. This is not a World Heritage inscription or a territorial boundary.

UCL's minute-precision coordinates for Lahun and Abydos were converted arithmetically. The Kerma point uses the published degrees, minutes and seconds. All are site references, not precise buildings or personal residences. The Senwosret III association locates a royal complex at Abydos, not the king's whereabouts throughout his reign. Amenemhat III retains a null personal map point.

The new link to Hammurabi compares overlapping dates with the Lahun records. It does not assert contact. Existing connections remain, and the new entries have onward links to their period, place or evidence context.

## Remaining gaps

No ancient boundaries, reconstructed itineraries or complete king lists were added. Kerma still needs more dated evidence about households and work. Egypt's southern forts deserve individual research rather than points inferred from a general frontier account. There are no new lifespan records in this batch: the royal entries remain reigns.
