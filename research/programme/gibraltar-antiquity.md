# Gibraltar in antiquity

24 November 2026. Four entries introduce Gibraltar, the sanctuary at Gorham’s Cave, its Egyptian scarabs and Mediterranean offerings. They connect the map to the existing Egypt area without implying direct journeys or named visitors.

## Evidence decisions

The cited government, museum, journal abstract and UNESCO passages were opened and inspected. The evidence records describe AI-assisted checking, not independent expert review.

- The Ministry for Heritage places Phoenician and Carthaginian use of the upper level between the ninth and third centuries BCE. The displayed 900–200 BCE window represents those centuries approximately, not known foundation and closure years.
- The Egyptian scarabs entry uses the museum’s ninth- to sixth-century phase attribution, displayed as 900–500 BCE. Later imitations are distinguished from these examples; the window does not date every scarab found in the cave.
- The inscriptions claim uses the Complutum abstract only. Phoenician and Graeco-Iberian examples are reported; association with votive offerings remains qualified. No detailed reading or individual inscription date is claimed.
- Pottery provenance does not establish a direct trade route or the donor’s origin. No route lines or foreign visitor locations have been invented.
- UNESCO’s property coordinate, N36 7 21.61 W5 20 31.42, is converted to decimal degrees as the cave-complex reference. Every entry and association identifies this limitation; it is not a precise findspot or a territorial boundary.
- The museum scarabs article misdates Avienus in its opening quotation. That passage and its proposed identification of the southern Pillar were not used.

## Scope

This is a starting collection for ancient Gibraltar and connections through the Strait. It does not claim complete regional coverage, include the much earlier Neanderthal evidence, or add unsupported city histories elsewhere around the Strait. The wider object connections can support later research into Gadir, Carthage and the Tangier peninsula.
