# Caesar and the end of Ptolemaic Egypt

Added 18 entries with 24 reviewed statements in `caesar-era-reviewed.json` and its exploration adapter. The batch connects Caesar, Pompey, Cicero, Antony, Cleopatra and Augustus; the campaigns in Gaul and Britain; the civil war; Alexandria; Actium; and Egypt’s transition to Roman rule. Sanchi’s gateways add a contemporary construction phase outside the Mediterranean. Existing Han, Parthian, Nubian, British, Irish and West African records remain available in the same window.

Lifespans are distinct from reigns. Caesar uses the conventional birth year 100 BCE, with the alternative 102 BCE noted. The Sanchi window represents a century-level attribution, not continuous building. The British expeditions are two visits, not continuous occupation.

Sources include museum publications, English Heritage, the Archaeological Survey of India, the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy and academic biographies. A few lifespan headings were checked through publicly indexed abstracts where the full page was inaccessible; the individual source locators explicitly record this limitation. UCL’s Cleopatra page is used for its reign heading only: its erroneous Actium date is not used. VRoma’s incorrect suggestion that 54 BCE was the first British crossing is not used; English Heritage supplies the two-expedition chronology. The claim that Caesar’s fire destroyed the entire Library of Alexandria is also not reproduced.

Dated map associations reuse existing city, regional or archaeological site reference points. They do not assert precise coordinates for a person. Cleopatra’s Egypt association describes the kingdom she ruled, not uninterrupted residence. Caesar’s Egyptian association is restricted to 48–47 BCE. Other parts of the protagonists’ itineraries remain unresearched.

Manual verification: four additional tests cover evidence and connections, the Egypt navigation chain, global contemporaries and exclusions, and time-limited location associations. No CI was added.
