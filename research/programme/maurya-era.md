# Mauryan expansion · 22 November 2026

Nine new entries and three expanded summaries connect South Asia to the
existing Alexander, Ptolemy and Sanchi material. The existing `maurya`,
`pataliputra` and `ashoka-rule` IDs are preserved. Reviewed data replaces
those starter summaries, rather than adding duplicate search results.

The batch contains 28 recorded statements, including date-scope and map
reference claims, with seven linked sources. These are AI-assisted source
checks, not independent expert review or a completed regional package.

## Source decisions

- The Met supplies the dynasty, capital and Ashoka chronology. Its title
  starts at 323 BCE while its main text uses 321; the entry follows the text.
- Livius supplies the conventional Chandragupta and Bindusara reign windows
  and Seleucus biography. Its disputed decline narrative is not adopted.
- Ashoka remains a reign entry, not a person with an invented birthday.
  The Met’s c.273–232 BCE range is retained; the alternative accession
  chronology is disclosed. Bindusara’s endpoint follows Livius and therefore
  overlaps the Met’s Ashoka opening; this is a chronology difference.
- The UCL Press synopsis of Sushma Jansari’s *Chandragupta Maurya* supports
  c.305–303 BCE for the settlement and explicitly leaves the military winner
  uncertain. The whole book has not been reviewed.
- UNESCO supports Lumbini, the 249 BCE visit and the site reference. Its
  traditional Buddha birth date is not adopted.
- UNESCO supports Taxila and Alexander’s entry in 326 BCE. Coordinates use
  the Bhir Mound component, not the entire serial property or a camp location.
- Pataliputra’s older, unsourced approximate point is not carried into the
  reviewed entry. Its city location still needs a checked reference.

The map records the dated visits only in their selected years. Period views
also display contemporary places, as elsewhere in the app; their markers
must not be read as a ruler’s continuous itinerary.

Source URLs, passage locators and review notes are in
`research/evidence/maurya-era-reviewed.json`. Search, reciprocal connections,
place switching and dated associations are covered by the batch tests.
