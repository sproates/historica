# Indus civilisation expansion

24 November 2026. This batch expands the existing Indus overview and adds Harappa, Mohenjo-daro, Dholavira, water storage at Dholavira, crafts at Harappa, seals and writing, and weights and trade. It adds places and onward journeys without treating regional coverage as complete.

## Evidence decisions

The cited passages were opened and inspected. Sources include UNESCO property descriptions, Getty's archaeological gazetteer, the Met chronology and Harappa Archaeological Research Project material. Claims identify the relevant passage and an AI-assisted review, not independent expert approval.

- The 2600–1900 BCE urban phase is an editorial comparison window for most entries. It is not each city's complete lifespan, a precise object date or proof of activity throughout every selected year.
- Dholavira retains UNESCO's broader 3000–1500 BCE occupation range. The water entry uses it as a study window, not the construction interval of every reservoir.
- Harappa uses Getty TGN 8039784, the deserted settlement, rather than TGN 7001540, the modern inhabited place. Its published decimal coordinates are retained. UNESCO tentative-list submission 1878 gives a substantially conflicting coordinate and was not used for the map.
- Mohenjo-daro and Dholavira use UNESCO property coordinates, converted arithmetically from degrees, minutes and seconds. These are site references, not workshops or houses.
- The Met's broad chronology is used for Mohenjo-daro rather than treating UNESCO's wider date range as precise settlement endpoints. UNESCO's older functional labels such as a priestly college and granary were not adopted.
- Kenoyer's craft research summary supports production and continuity, but no particular workshop floor or individual craftsperson is located.
- The seal HR 743 supplies an identifiable example from Mohenjo-daro. No reading of its signs or identification of its owner is claimed.
- The possible use of weights in trade regulation and taxation remains an interpretation. Western trading connections do not provide an exact route or a named transaction.

All published entries have their own claim references. The overview retains no arbitrary central point; its three dated city associations provide geographical context. Connections with Khufu are comparisons of dates, not evidence of direct contact.

## Remaining work

This batch does not cover the whole Indus settlement network, agriculture, changing river systems or the later regional transformations. It makes no single-cause claim about urban decline and adds no reconstructed state boundaries or lists of invented rulers.
