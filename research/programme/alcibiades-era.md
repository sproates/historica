# Alcibiades and the Peloponnesian War

Added 22 entries and 42 reviewed statements. The new people are Alcibiades, Pericles and Nicias. Sparta and Syracuse are clickable city focuses with sourced orientation points. The wider sequence includes the Peloponnesian War, epidemic at Athens, Potidaea deployment, Delium, Peace of Nicias, Sicilian expedition, religious scandal of 415, Alcibiades’ Spartan cooperation and return, the Four Hundred, the Arginusae trial, Aegospotami, Athens’ surrender, the Thirty and restoration of democracy. Darius II’s reign and Amyrtaeus’ accession connect the war to Persia and Egypt.

The adapter connects the existing Socrates record to Alcibiades and to military and civic episodes. It does not duplicate Socrates or his trial. Existing global contemporary records remain visible. Athens, Rome and Persia have broader editorial default windows so their newly researched histories are included when opening those places directly; changing focus to a place still preserves the incoming period.

Chronological choices:

- Alcibiades uses approximately 450–404 BCE; the source alternatives 451/450 and 404/403 are stated. Pericles and Nicias have approximate births. Darius II is represented by a reign, not an invented lifespan.
- The 432 Potidaea entry dates deployment only. It does not compress the whole siege or date the rescue story to a particular battle.
- Alcibiades’ recall and flight use 415 BCE, following the war chronology rather than the 414 placement in the Stanford Socrates chronology. His return to Athens is 407, distinct from his earlier return to command. Notium is not given an entry because reconciling its 407/406 chronology remains outside this batch.
- The epidemic is a series of outbreaks in 430–426, without a claimed settled diagnosis. Literary accounts of Socrates at Delium are labelled as such.
- The Four Hundred of 411 and the Thirty of 404–403 are separate regimes. Socrates’ trial in 399 is connected to the restored democracy without asserting that Alcibiades’ career was its sole cause. Livius’ concluding revenge interpretation is not reproduced.
- Amyrtaeus’ approximate accession in 404 is not treated as immediate control over all Egypt. The Cambridge summary documents continued recognition of the Persian king at Elephantine in 401. No disputed end-of-reign date is required by this event.

Getty TGN supplies Sparta and Acropolis coordinates. The UNESCO nomination’s indexed identification page supplies Ortygia’s coordinates for Syracuse; the entire large PDF was not available in the web reader. Acropolis points locate Athens broadly, not the exact setting of trials or political events. Alcibiades receives only dated associations for his flight to Sparta and return to Athens, not a made-up complete itinerary.
