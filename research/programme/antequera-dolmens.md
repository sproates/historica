# Antequera dolmens

24 November 2026. Four entries add an overview and separate construction histories for Menga, Viera and El Romeral. Andalucía’s study window now begins at 3800 BCE, and its map gains dated references for the monuments. A comparison link leads to Stonehenge without implying contact.

## Evidence decisions

- Menga uses the approximate 3800–3600 BCE construction dating in Lozano Rodríguez and colleagues’ 2024 Science Advances paper. The abstract and introduction were inspected in the University of Granada copy. Its construction model is described as an interpretation.
- Viera follows the 2016 ICOMOS assessment: the last third of the fourth millennium BCE, rounded to 3330–3000 BCE for display. Later possible funerary or religious activity is distinguished from construction.
- El Romeral retains the broad 3300–2200 BCE attribution in that assessment. The assessment explicitly lacks finer dating; the timeline must not be read as a construction duration. This is an attributed published range, not a claim that research has settled the date.
- The overview’s 3800–2200 BCE window combines those ranges. It does not imply uninterrupted work or define the monuments’ full histories.
- UNESCO’s component 1501-001 provides one shared coordinate for Menga and Viera. Both entries disclose that limitation instead of inventing separate precise points. Component 1501-002 supplies El Romeral’s reference. Coordinates were converted from degrees, minutes and seconds.
- UNESCO’s description supports architecture, ritual and funerary use, and landscape orientation. No named builders, beliefs or political boundaries are invented.
- Menga predates Stonehenge’s first earthwork on the cited chronologies. The comparison claim includes both sources; it does not imply simultaneous construction or cultural transmission.

The overview covers the three monuments named in the World Heritage property. It does not claim to exhaust Antequera’s prehistoric landscape or include the more recently researched Piedras Blancas monument. No images or lengthy source passages have been copied.
