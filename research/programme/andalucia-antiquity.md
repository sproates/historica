# Andalucía in antiquity

24 November 2026. A first regional collection adds Andalucía, Gadir, Baelo Claudia and Itálica. Gadir connects to the offerings already recorded at Gorham’s Cave, and Itálica connects to Rome. The area page gets dated associations with all three sites rather than an arbitrary central map point.

## Evidence decisions

The municipal, national tourism and heritage sources were opened and inspected. Claims record AI-assisted source checking, not independent expert review.

- Andalucía is a modern geographical grouping. No ancient state boundary is implied. The 900 BCE–200 CE area window is editorial.
- Gadir’s 900–500 BCE window follows the municipal account’s ninth-century houses and sixth-century fire. It is not the full lifespan of Cádiz. Traditional foundation dates are not presented as archaeological facts. The visitor narrative’s named character, Mattan, is not added as a historical person.
- Baelo Claudia’s late second-century BCE origins use 125 BCE as an approximate display anchor. Its 200 CE endpoint is the collection limit, not an abandonment date. No exact date for each surviving installation is inferred.
- Itálica’s 206 BCE Roman foundation is distinguished from the pre-existing Turdetanian settlement and the second-century CE northern extension. The source is Spain’s tentative-list submission, not a claim that the site is inscribed on the World Heritage List. Its 200 CE endpoint also reflects collection scope.
- Gadir’s point is the embedded archaeological-site marker on Turespaña’s page. Itálica uses the coordinate in Spain’s heritage submission and identifies the Hadrianic sector as the reference.
- Turespaña’s Baelo page has a misplaced embedded marker, around 15 km east of the ruins. That coordinate was rejected. The PATRIF site record supplies the published decimal reference, 36.09029, -5.77485. Its rounded degrees/minutes/seconds representation differs slightly; the decimal value is retained, without claiming an exact factory findspot.
- Gadir pottery at Gorham’s Cave supports movement of objects, not an identified visitor, direct route or political relationship.

## Remaining coverage

This first collection does not cover all of Andalucía. Tartessian and Iberian communities, the region’s earlier prehistory, other Phoenician settlements and the wider Roman countryside remain to be researched. Medieval al-Andalus lies outside the current ancient-history window.
