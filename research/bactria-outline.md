# Bactria outline interpretation

Added 22 November 2026. The working map now includes an original, coarse sketch of the southern heartland. Its label and legend identify it as approximate. It is not a reconstruction of the complete satrapy or a frontier proven for 329 BCE.

The [Barrington Atlas commentary, pp. 1359–1360](https://assets.press.princeton.edu/B_ATLAS/BATL098_.pdf) identifies the heartland south of the Oxus and discusses evidence for northern Bactria beyond the river. [Bruno Jacobs, Achaemenid Satrapies, section 7.1.1](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/achaemenid-satrapies/) describes the Hindu Kush and Oxus as boundary features, while acknowledging roughly determined eastern and western limits. These interpretations do not establish exact polygon vertices.

## Construction and limits

The outline is an editorial interpretation of those geographical descriptions. Coarse vertices loosely follow modern Amu Darya/Panj reference geography to the north and the Hindu Kush zone to the south. East and west closures are illustrative. No published map image was traced. Northern Bactria is deliberately omitted; ancient river courses are not reconstructed. Display during 330–326 BCE is a prototype scope choice, not evidence that boundaries stayed unchanged.

The original polygon is dedicated under CC0. Its coordinates, method, citations and limitations are in `web/data/collection.json`, under `historicalOverlays`. This attribution does not grant reuse rights to the cited publications. The polygon has not had independent historical review.

## Behaviour checked

The shaded outline remains visible at both endpoints of the modern-border slider. Clicking it, or selecting “About this outline”, opens its interpretation and sources. The data check confirms that the polygon closes, includes Bactra, and excludes Kabul and Samarkand. These checks catch implementation mistakes; they do not validate historical accuracy.
