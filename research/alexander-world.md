# Alexander and contemporary world context

Prototype extension, 22 November 2026. The application now separates selected year, campaign stage, selected record and map camera. Follow mode centres the map on the chosen stage. Explore mode preserves the camera when time changes, with Alexander’s stage and estimated age retained above the map.

## Collection scope

Fifteen main stages cover 336–323 BCE, from accession in Macedonia to death at Babylon. The list includes Greece, Asia Minor, the Levant, Egypt, Mesopotamia, Persia, Bactria, Sogdiana, Punjab, the Indus, Gedrosia and the return west. This is a campaign overview, not an exhaustive itinerary. Several years have more than one stage; use the stage controls to visit them separately.

The primary chronology used is the [Cambridge University Press campaign table](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/AA5F32E13E20CAFF2ACE09749527E515/9781009408981chr_xiv-xvii.pdf/chronology.pdf), supplemented by [Mark Rose’s chronology for the Archaeological Institute of America](https://archive.archaeology.org/online/features/alexander/whoswho.html). The original Iranica evidence remains attached to the Bactria arrival. Content consists of short original summaries, not copied article text.

Stage reference coordinates are deliberately rounded editorial positions for the named places or broad regions. No exact battlefield or continuous personal position is asserted. Dashed lines connect the sequence of stages only. They must not be exported or described as reconstructed roads, travel corridors or conquered borders. The existing Bactria heartland sketch remains a separate geographical interpretation, shown within its original study window.

## Elsewhere at the same time

- **China:** Warring States background, following the [Getty Research Institute](https://www.getty.edu/vow/AATFullDisplay?find=300264024&logic=AND&note=&subjectid=300018378). Its 403–221 BCE dating is retained, with the alternative 475 BCE starting date acknowledged. The marker is regional, not a capital.
- **Italy:** background after the Latin War, followed from 326 BCE by the Second Samnite War. Evidence comes from [Tenney Frank’s A History of Rome, chapter IV](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/secondary/FRAAHR/4*.html). This is an older synthesis; the summaries use basic chronology, not its evaluative language. The switch between cards is editorial presentation, not a newly inferred historical boundary.
- **Mesoamerica:** early Monte Albán, using the approximate 500–100 BCE archaeological phase described by [Secretaría de Cultura / INAH](https://sic.cultura.gob.mx/ficha.php?table=zona_arqueologica&table_id=76). The card describes period context and does not assign individual buildings or monuments to Alexander’s selected year. One site cannot stand for all the Americas.

These four context records do not constitute comprehensive regional coverage. Their date intervals are checked for overlap with the selected year. The local list also checks whether the record’s reference point lies within the map viewport. Empty map areas explicitly indicate absent coverage.

## Age and shared views

Alexander’s birth year is stored as 356 BCE. The displayed age range allows for an unknown birthday within the selected year. His death year displays a final-year label instead of implying survival beyond his death. Exact days and months are not supplied by this fixture.

Shared URLs retain year, stage, selected context or place, follow/explore mode, map position and border opacity. Existing Bactria URLs remain readable. An empty selection is explicitly encoded so that a reload does not select an unrelated distant event.

## Verification

Automated checks cover the two navigation modes, year coverage, overlapping contexts, Italy’s change of context, empty coverage, shared URLs, age ranges and source references. Browser checks cover jumping to other regions without changing date, advancing time while retaining the map position, returning to the campaign, reloading a shared view and the narrow-screen layout.
