At Bolonia, near Tarifa, Baelo Claudia preserves the streets and working spaces of a Roman coastal town. Its origins are placed in the late second century BCE. Main streets met at the forum, beside buildings used for civic administration, while the theatre took advantage of the hillside.

Fish processing was part of the town’s economy. The surviving salting installations sit alongside the remains of a market, baths and water supply. Together they let us compare everyday production with the public spaces through which the town was organised.
