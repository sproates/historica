At Kuk in the New Guinea highlands, people cultivated plants on raised mounds along wetland margins. Research dates this phase to about 5000–4490 BCE and identifies intensive banana cultivation.

The evidence supports an independent origin of agriculture in New Guinea. This was a different setting and farming tradition from those developing around the Nile or in Mesopotamia.
