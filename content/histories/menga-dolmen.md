Menga was built around 3800–3600 BCE, long before Stonehenge’s first earthwork. Its builders moved and fitted enormous stones: a 2024 study estimates the largest roof slab at about 150 tonnes.

Three surviving pillars run along the chamber’s central axis. The study examines the stones and foundations to propose how the structure was assembled. That reconstruction is an archaeological interpretation, rather than a surviving account from its builders.
