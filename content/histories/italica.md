Itálica grew beside an existing Turdetanian settlement near the Guadalquivir. Spain’s heritage submission dates its Roman foundation to 206 BCE, when Scipio settled veterans after the battle of Ilipa. Its remains lie at Santiponce, northwest of Seville.

The city changed substantially in the second century CE, particularly under Hadrian. A large extension added broad streets and public buildings to the north of the earlier settlement. The monumental remains therefore belong to different stages of the city’s history, rather than a single founding plan.

Trajan was born at Itálica, and Hadrian’s family came from there. Those connections help explain why a city in southern Iberia features in the history of the wider Roman Empire.
