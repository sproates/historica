Mohenjo-daro’s baked-brick buildings, wells and drains make the practical organisation of an Indus city unusually visible. Streets meet at right angles, and a raised western area stands beside the lower city.

Its objects offer a closer scale of comparison. One seal from the site carries eight signs above a one-horned animal. We can describe the material and image, but the writing cannot yet be read with confidence. Follow the seals entry to see how those limits affect what we can say.
