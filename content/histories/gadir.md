Beneath Cádiz’s puppet theatre, excavations reveal part of the Phoenician settlement of Gadir. Eight houses stood on two terraces beside streets surfaced with clay. The city council’s account dates the early houses and streets to the ninth century BCE.

These remains bring the settlement down to the scale of homes and daily movement: even cattle footprints survive in the street surfaces. The excavated sequence also includes evidence of a sixth-century BCE fire. This entry follows those early levels, rather than treating them as the whole lifespan of Cádiz.

Pottery attributed to Gadir and its surroundings was also left at Gorham’s Cave in Gibraltar. That is evidence of objects moving through the region, without identifying their carriers or an exact route.
