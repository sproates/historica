Egypt was reunited under Mentuhotep II after a period of divided rule. Under the Twelfth Dynasty, the royal centre moved north to Itjtawi and kings again built pyramid complexes. The surviving buildings, objects and writing show changes in government, religion and the way kings presented themselves.

Egypt’s southern frontier was also a place of movement and exchange. Forts regulated Nubian traders and supported mining and military operations. Further south, Kerma had its own rulers and economy. These histories overlap, but Egyptian expansion should not be mistaken for control of the whole Nile valley.

For a closer view, follow the records from Lahun or the religious life of Abydos. Both let us look beyond a sequence of royal reigns.
