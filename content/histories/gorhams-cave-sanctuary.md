People visiting Gorham’s Cave left offerings in a coastal shrine beneath the Rock of Gibraltar. The Ministry for Heritage dates its Phoenician and Carthaginian use to the ninth through third centuries BCE, interpreting the offerings as requests for safe passage.

Some pottery also carries inscriptions or incised marks. Archaeologists have identified Phoenician and Graeco-Iberian examples and suggest that they may relate to votive offerings. The marks add another kind of evidence to the objects themselves, without giving us a full account of who visited or what each person believed.
