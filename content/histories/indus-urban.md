The Indus civilisation included large cities in what are now Pakistan and northwestern India. During its urban phase, roughly 2600–1900 BCE, Harappa and Mohenjo-daro were connected with settlements and trading partners beyond their own region. Pottery, metalworking, beads and carved seals are among the surviving evidence.

These cities did not all look alike. Dholavira, in Gujarat, offers a different setting, where storing water was central to urban life. Explore the individual sites to compare their buildings and the objects recovered from them.

The signs on Indus seals remain undeciphered. That limits what we can say about the words and identities behind the objects, even where their material and findspot are known.
