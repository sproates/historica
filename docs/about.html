ABOUT HISTORICA

History doesn’t happen
one place at a time.

We often learn history in separate pieces: a civilisation, a ruler, a war. Historica brings those pieces alongside one another.

Made for curious people

What was happening in China while Socrates was alive? Who was a contemporary of Cleopatra? Where was Bactria, and how does it relate to the map we know today?

Historica starts with questions like these. Choose a person, place, period or event, then explore the same years elsewhere. The timeline gives you the comparison; the connections and map give you somewhere to go next.

It is a way into history, rather than an encyclopaedia or an academic reference. The aim is to make relationships easier to see and give you sources to follow when you want to learn more.

A growing collection

The ambition is to explore human history across the world. For now, the collection concentrates on roughly 3000 BCE to 200 CE, with earlier predynastic material and some context beyond those dates.

Coverage is uneven. Some entries have detailed, linked evidence; others are provisional. An empty timeline or map is a gap in the collection, not a claim that nothing was happening there.

Dates can be approximate, disputed or specific to a particular source. Historical areas are not always precise boundaries. Each entry’s notes explain these limitations where they are known.

Still taking shape

Historica is in beta. The interface, historical material and ways of exploring it are still developing.

Research and source checks use AI assistance; they are not independent expert review. Linked sources let you inspect the basis for an entry yourself.

The plan is to make Historica open source. The code repository is private while the prototype takes shape.
